# Water purification

Making water safe to drink in the field comes down to three approaches:
boiling, filtering, and chemical treatment. Most situations call for more
than one.

## Boiling

Boiling is the most reliable method and needs no special equipment beyond a
container and heat. Bring the water to a rolling boil and hold it there for
one full minute. Above two thousand metres, hold the boil for three minutes,
because water boils at a lower temperature as altitude rises and needs
longer to reach the same effect. Let it cool covered so nothing falls in.

## Filtering

Filtering removes sediment, protozoa and most bacteria, but not viruses.
Pour cloudy water through tightly woven cloth before any other treatment;
suspended sediment shields pathogens from both heat and chemicals. A ceramic
or hollow-fibre filter rated to 0.2 microns handles giardia and
cryptosporidium.

## Chemical treatment

Iodine and chlorine dioxide tablets are light and need no fuel. Chlorine
dioxide takes thirty minutes for most pathogens and four hours for
cryptosporidium. Iodine is ineffective against cryptosporidium entirely and
should not be used by anyone pregnant or with a thyroid condition.
