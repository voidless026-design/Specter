# Antibiotics and resistance

Antibiotics act on bacteria and have no effect whatsoever on viruses, which
is why they do nothing for a cold or influenza.

## Mechanisms

Beta-lactams such as penicillin disrupt cell wall synthesis. Macrolides and
tetracyclines interfere with the bacterial ribosome. Fluoroquinolones target
DNA replication. Human cells have different machinery at each of these
points, which is what makes selective toxicity possible.

## Resistance

Resistance arises through mutation and through horizontal gene transfer,
where bacteria pass resistance genes between species on plasmids. Finishing
a prescribed course matters less than once taught; current evidence favours
the shortest effective course, because every additional day selects for
resistant strains.

## Broad and narrow spectrum

A narrow-spectrum antibiotic targeting the known organism does less damage
to the rest of the microbiome and selects less strongly for resistance.
Broad-spectrum agents exist for when the organism is unknown and the
situation is urgent.
