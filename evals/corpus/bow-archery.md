# The bow

A bow stores energy in its limbs and transfers it to an arrow. The design
has barely changed in principle for ten thousand years.

## Types

A longbow is a single curved stave, traditionally yew, and is the simplest
to make. A recurve curves away from the archer at the tips, storing more
energy for the same draw length. A compound bow uses cams to hold most of
the draw weight at full draw, which is why a compound archer can aim for
longer.

## Draw weight

Draw weight is measured in pounds at a given draw length, conventionally 28
inches. A 45-pound recurve is a reasonable hunting weight; anything much
heavier is difficult to shoot accurately without practice.

## The string

Bowstrings are made of many strands laid parallel and served in the middle
where the arrow nocks. A bow left strung loses draw weight over months as
the limbs take a set, so unstring it for storage.
