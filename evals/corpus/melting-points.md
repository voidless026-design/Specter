# Melting points of common materials

The melting point is the temperature at which a solid becomes liquid at
standard atmospheric pressure. For alloys and mixtures it is usually a range
rather than a point.

## Metals

Mercury melts at minus 39 degrees Celsius and is liquid at room temperature.
Tin melts at 232, lead at 327, aluminium at 660, copper at 1085, and iron at
1538. Solder is chosen for melting below the components it joins.

## Non-metals

Ice melts at 0 by definition of the Celsius scale. Paraffin wax softens
between 46 and 68 depending on the blend. Window glass has no true melting
point; it softens gradually from around 550.

## Why it varies

Stronger bonds mean higher melting points. Metallic bonding strength rises
with the number of delocalised electrons per atom, which is broadly why
transition metals melt higher than alkali metals.
