# Tungsten

Tungsten is a chemical element with the symbol W and atomic number 74. The
symbol comes from wolfram, its German name; tungsten itself is Swedish for
heavy stone.

## Properties

Tungsten has the highest melting point of all metals at 3422 degrees Celsius
and the highest tensile strength of any pure metal. Its density is 19.3
grams per cubic centimetre, close to that of gold. In pure form it is
surprisingly soft and can be cut with a hacksaw; impurities make it brittle.

## Uses

Tungsten filaments made incandescent lighting practical and remain in use in
specialist lamps. Tungsten carbide, a compound rather than the metal, is
used for cutting tools and abrasives and is nearly as hard as diamond. The
metal's density makes it a substitute for lead in ammunition and for
radiation shielding.

## History

Identified as a new element in 1781 by Carl Wilhelm Scheele and isolated in
1783 by the brothers Juan Jose and Fausto Elhuyar.
