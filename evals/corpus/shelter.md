# Emergency shelter

Exposure kills faster than thirst or hunger. A shelter has one job: to keep
you out of wind and rain and off cold ground.

## Siting

Avoid valley floors, where cold air pools overnight, and ridgelines, where
wind is worst. Look up before committing to a spot - dead branches overhead
fall without warning and are called widowmakers for good reason. Avoid dry
creek beds entirely; they flood fast.

## Lean-to

A lean-to is the fastest effective shelter. Lash a ridgepole between two
trees at waist height, lay branches against it at an angle, and thatch with
debris from the bottom up so water sheds downward. Aim for a foot of
material.

## Insulation

Ground conduction takes more heat than air. Pile dry leaves, pine boughs or
grass at least six inches thick underneath you before worrying about the
roof.
