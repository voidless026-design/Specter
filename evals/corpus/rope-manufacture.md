# Rope manufacture

Rope is made by twisting fibres into yarns, yarns into strands, and strands
into rope, each stage in the opposite direction to the last. That opposition
is what keeps the rope together under load.

## Natural fibre

Manila, sisal and hemp were the standard for centuries. They grip well and
are pleasant to handle but rot when stored wet and lose strength
unpredictably with age.

## Synthetic

Nylon stretches considerably and absorbs shock, which makes it right for
climbing and anchoring. Polyester stretches far less and holds up better in
sunlight. Polypropylene floats and is cheap but weakens badly with UV
exposure.

## Breaking strain

Working load is conventionally a fifth of breaking strain or less. Knots
reduce strength substantially - a bowline costs roughly a third - because
the fibres on the outside of the bend carry more load than those inside.
