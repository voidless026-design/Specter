# Fire making

A fire needs three things in the right proportion: tinder that catches a
spark, kindling that catches from tinder, and fuel that burns long enough to
be useful. Most failed fires are a kindling problem.

## Tinder

Dry grass, birch bark, cattail fluff and the inner bark of cedar all take a
spark. Birch bark works even when wet because of the oils in it. Cotton wool
rubbed with petroleum jelly is the most reliable prepared tinder and burns
for several minutes.

## Ignition

A ferrocerium rod throws sparks at around three thousand degrees Celsius and
works when soaked, which matches are not. Scrape it with a sharp spine
rather than a blade edge. A magnifying lens works in direct sun and costs
nothing to carry.

## Building up

Lay a platform of dry sticks so the tinder is not sitting on wet ground.
Build kindling in a loose cone or lean-to so air can move through it.
Crowding a young fire with heavy fuel smothers it.
